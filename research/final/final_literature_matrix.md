# Final literature evidence matrix

Research date: 2026-09-10. Primary papers, official repositories/model cards, and survey documentation were searched. No external respondent data was downloaded. Internal-history comparisons were reconciled against executed notebooks and primary result artifacts; the experiment ledger in `final_day_audit.md` is authoritative. Expected upside and compute labels are engineering judgments, not measured QWK gains.

## Dataset identity and limitations of comparable evidence

Raw competition `data/train_final.csv` contains **95,609 rows and 179 columns including target**. Its observed `survey_year`/`data_year` values are **2021–2024**, with missing values and agreement where both are present. The translated dictionary has the four target categories very satisfied, satisfied, dissatisfied, very dissatisfied; pseudo strata/PSU, sample-adult flags, annual income imputations, and NHIS-like health, disability, insurance, mental-health and food-security modules. These are strong **survey-family identification evidence** for redesigned NHIS, not proof of an exact public respondent mapping. A presumed 2019–2023 origin would contradict the observed year columns. Do not obtain external microdata to resolve it.

The [official 2021 NHIS adult codebook](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2021/adult-codebook.pdf) documents the four-category item and the separate eleven-category item, a randomized order flag, and distinct refused/not-ascertained/don't-know codes. The competition dictionary is simplified: for example, some functional difficulty questions are described as yes/no and anxiety severity codes are not numerically ordered by severity. The actual observed values and official question universes must govern semantic encoding. Do not assume every 7/8/9 or 97/98/99 is special without considering the particular feature's legitimate range.

The [2021 survey description](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/148808/cdc_148808_DS1.pdf) describes a randomized rotation beginning in Q2: life-satisfaction questions could occur early after general health or later after social support. This establishes a questionnaire-context mechanism; it does **not** establish that a supplied ID, year or missingness pattern reveals random assignment. Year/quarter interactions may be tested on raw train, while reconstructing an absent assignment is unjustified. The [2022 survey documentation](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2022-nhis.html) supplies authoritative questionnaires and flowcharts for checking year-specific availability without respondent data.

| Population study / year | Dataset and analysis | Observed result | Credibility and modeling implication |
|---|---|---|---|

Reported SHAP shares are conditional on its selected model, not achievable competition importance. Social support and full distress instruments are not in the supplied dictionary; related proxies cannot recreate absent covariates. |

Not a same-split prediction result; no comparable QWK ceiling. |
| [Lund et al., 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0091743521002930) | 2017 BRFSS in three states, N=25,850; stratified prevalence contrasts | Emotional support moderated associations of housing/food/financial insecurity with dissatisfaction; mental distress also modified some associations | Direct support for considering burden × protection, but observational, binary outcome and different survey. Do not import effect sizes or call marriage an equivalent measure of support. |

| [Lee et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27138962/) | Randomized web survey question-context experiments; first experiment N=648 | Studies order sensitivity of life satisfaction, self-rated health and subjective life expectancy | Supports treating self-report context as a possible source of response noise; small separate experiments cannot quantify this competition's ceiling. |

**No rigorous same-input, same-split four-class QWK result was found.** Literature cannot justify a numerical ceiling above 0.50682, nor declare it impossible. Associations predicting the rare dissatisfied group provide little direct evidence for distinguishing very satisfied from satisfied. A 95k-row training set addresses sampling error more than missing emotional-support, personality, wording/context and subjective-adaptation information.

The 2026 SSL evaluation is a recent preprint with 14 tasks, not a universal theorem: average gains over scratch were not statistically significant in its tested label-scarcity conditions. It supports caution, while V15's actual competition result is the stronger rejection evidence. Conversely RealMLP's benchmark claims concern aggregated scores on multiple tasks, not an attainable life-satisfaction accuracy.

## Ordinal objective and calibration comparison

| Method | Primary evidence | Fit to this error regime | Failure/risk and verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative link / all-threshold | [Pedregosa et al., consistency of ordinal methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2327) | Three events `Y>1`, `Y>2`, `Y>3`; expected class is `1 + sum(P(Y>k))` | Consistency for particular pointwise losses is not QWK optimality. Independent heads can cross; shared proportional-odds score can underfit boundary-specific effects. Good reference objective. |
| CORAL | [Cao et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07884) | Shared logits plus ordered biases enforce consistent ordinal ranks | Sharing constrains representations; age-estimation gains are not four-class survey evidence. No reason to revive merely for ordinal terminology. |
| CORN | [Shi et al., conditional ordinal probabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08851) | Conditional training and multiplication give ordered survival probabilities without shared output weights | Later heads train on fewer rows and errors propagate; sparse class 4 matters. V6 weak result penalizes rerun. |
| Soft four-class distribution / hierarchical factorization | Probability chain rule; CORN source above | With `t=P(Y>=3)`, `a=P(Y=2 | Y<=2)`, `b=P(Y=4 | Y>=3)`, use `[(1-t)(1-a),(1-t)a,t(1-b),tb]`; mean `1+a+t(2+b-a)` | Algebraically coherent, but not proof of better learning. All heads require training labels; never hard-route rows or fit gate weights to held-out test diagnostics. |
| Conditional distributional regression | [NGBoost, ICML 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03225) | Model dispersion as well as mean for heterogeneous response uncertainty | Continuous likelihood assumptions can be poor for four spikes. Four-class CE probabilities already provide a discrete distribution; no extra bins needed. |
| CDF / squared-EMD loss | [Hou et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05916) | Sum squared differences of predicted and target cumulative probabilities uses order | This cumulative-square loss is not generally identical to squared Wasserstein-2 and is not QWK. A small auxiliary loss is a testable design, not a guaranteed tail cure. |
| Differentiable weighted kappa | [de la Torre et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167865517301666) | Explicit weighted disagreement ratio | Batch marginal estimates couple examples; tiny/sparse tail batches make optimization unstable. Can sacrifice calibrated probabilities. Only a fixed, predeclared auxiliary ablation with QWK and AUC checks. |
| Pairwise / ranking loss | [RankNet, 2005](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/learning-to-rank-using-gradient-descent/) | Targets within-boundary ordering independent of cuts | Pair sampling changes weighting, full pairs are quadratic, and ranking does not determine class probabilities. Consider only after threshold-free ranking deficit is train-confirmed. |
| Focal / imbalance reweighting | [Lin et al., ICCV 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02002) | Emphasizes hard examples | Original evidence is dense object detection; hard survey responses may be noise. No directly comparable ordinal-focal result was established. Avoid arbitrary inverse-frequency weights that overcall tails. |
| Temperature / held-out calibration | [Guo et al., ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html); [nested evaluation documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_nested_cross_validation_iris.html) | Separates score calibration from representation learning | Better log-loss calibration is not necessarily better QWK. Every outer fold requires cuts/temperature/blend choice from an inner labeled partition only. Optimizing global OOF cuts then reporting that OOF is optimistic. |

QWK is `1 - observed weighted disagreement / expected weighted disagreement`, where expected disagreement depends on both marginal label distributions. Consequently reduced mean squared label error can coexist with a worse denominator, and stronger boundary AUC can coexist with worse QWK. Report both. With calibrated class probabilities `p`, each candidate label has conditional expected squared cost `sum_k p_k (k-j)^2`; any kappa-aware decision rule must estimate its marginal/ratio terms entirely within training calibration data. Do not substitute hidden class proportions. This is mathematical reasoning, not an externally measured improvement.

## Pretrained provenance and release audit

The [official Mitra-v2 classifier card](https://huggingface.co/autogluon/mitra-classifier-2) explicitly states synthetic-only pretraining and Apache-2.0; the [regressor card](https://huggingface.co/autogluon/mitra-regressor-2) gives the analogous claim. Metadata were read from the Hugging Face model API, **not weights**, on the research date:

| Repository | Exact observed revision | Public state | Parameter metadata |
|---|---|---|---|
| `autogluon/mitra-classifier-2` | `edada0d20759c58ada8c8605c25f22f6e98ea5f0` | Public, ungated, Apache-2.0; modified 2026-09-07 | 75,670,026 FP32 parameters; `config.json`, `model.safetensors` |
| `autogluon/mitra-regressor-2` | `246c7d310fff04296a2388df5f448e3ffe809283` | Public, ungated, Apache-2.0; modified 2026-09-08 | 76,684,776 FP32 parameters; `config.json`, `model.safetensors` |

This materially reduces respondent-overlap concerns, conditional on the producer's provenance statement. It is not independent reconstruction of pretraining and does not substitute for checking the competition's exact rule. No model is selected merely because its weights are legal.

The [Mitra-v2 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04540v1) evaluates 51 TabArena datasets (816 split units) and a matched-context 274-dataset TALENT board. Classification uses ROC-AUC error/log-loss, not QWK. Published deployment fine-tunes and bags eight models, unlike zero-shot comparisons. Pretraining has up to 5,120 support rows and 50 features; **50 is not a downstream feature limit**. Deployment allows a 256-feature budget, with dtype-gated reduction for wider continuous tables, and up to 32,768 multiclass prediction support rows. These facts make the 176-feature problem admissible in shape but do not certify T4 speed. More-than-ten-class performance is especially irrelevant to this four-class target. Benchmark Elo is pool-dependent and cannot be converted to a QWK delta.

The [official fine-tuning wrapper](https://huggingface.co/autogluon/mitra-finetune/blob/main/README.md) requires CUDA, Python 3.11–3.13, AutoGluon ≥1.6 and TabArena. Flash attention is optional but materially affects throughput. Its documentation says prediction calls run a bagged fine-tune; treating repeated calls as cheap inference would mis-budget the notebook. Any implementation would need an exact code revision, audited held-out-support behavior and a tiny real T4 smoke test. The available evidence does not justify introducing this dependency stack on the final day without that test.

RealMLP and TabM trained from random initialization avoid pretrained respondent-overlap questions. Pin package versions, record repository/license metadata, fit preprocessors only inside folds and preserve unknown-category behavior. Using their names for custom approximations does not inherit their benchmark evidence.

## Decision implications

1. Preserve the strongest GBDT anchor and all distinct observed survey states. Coding alone is not new information; deterministic summaries are alternative inductive bias and must earn their cost in OOF.
2. The most defensible open representation test from literature is **one full-target supervised RealMLP or TabM branch**, compared with a fresh anchor and admitted only through nested calibration and paired train OOF. The literature does not select between them without implementation/resource evidence.
3. Protective moderation is epidemiologically plausible, but no supplied proxy is proven to capture emotional support, and no literature result establishes a new resilience head. Only train-supported conditional residual gains justify specialists.
4. Reject reconstruction-first, historical prediction stacking and repeated foundation-model screens that retain the same learning problem. Mitra-v2 is a legitimate new prior but has higher final-day engineering risk than the supervised candidates.
5. No numerical QWK breakthrough is supported by external benchmarks. Crossing 0.50682 remains an unverified ambition to be accepted or rejected by leakage-safe competition training evidence, not by model-card rankings.
